# AI-Led Retail Analytics Experiment: Note for Professor Daily

After completing Drive Time Analytics through a highly collaborative, step-by-step process, I wanted to try the opposite approach. I provided a second fictional retail dataset to Microsoft Copilot and deliberately handed off as much of the analytical work as possible. My goal was to see how much AI could complete independently, what still required me as the analyst, how the workflow changed, and how quickly we could reach a finished interactive project.

For the second project, I did not select the business question, correlations, statistical tests, models, feature priorities, executive findings, or Streamlit layout. Copilot handled those analytical and production decisions. My involvement was primarily defining the experiment, supplying the fictional dataset, saving requested artifacts, creating and authenticating the GitHub repository, deploying the prepared application through my Streamlit account, and verifying that the public application worked.

The most interesting result was not that AI could build an application quickly. It was how my role changed. In Drive Time, much of my time was spent learning tools, troubleshooting, building, questioning, and making incremental decisions. In the AI-led project, the production work moved largely to AI, leaving the human role centered more on purpose, context, skepticism, validation, ownership, and accountability.

I do not think the experiment shows that the analyst is unnecessary. In fact, completing Drive Time first gave me a much better basis for judging the second project. Without learning the analytical process myself, I would have had less ability to evaluate whether an AI-produced result was reasonable.

There is also an important limitation to the comparison: the two fictional datasets are structurally different. Drive Time contained broad daily operational measures with relatively weak predictive relationships, while the second dataset contains transaction-level price, quantity, discount, sales, cost, and profit relationships. The model results therefore should not be treated as a controlled comparison of human versus AI analytical performance.

Live AI-led project: https://ai-led-retail-analytics.streamlit.app/

All data used in both projects are fictional and synthetic.
